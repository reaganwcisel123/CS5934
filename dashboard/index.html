Triad Signal · Clinic Needs Atlas

See unmet health need before it becomes a hospitalization.

The Clinic Needs Atlas turns public data into a working map of where Virginia's rural communities need care the most, so small clinics can reach the right people first.

Open the atlas See how it works

Every Virginia locality gets a 0 to 100 unmet-need score built from federal health and social data, so a clinic can see who is at risk, why, and what to do next.

Three questions, answered in one place

The atlas exists to answer the three questions every rural clinic asks about preventable hospitalizations.

Who

Calibrated risk tiers

A statistical model scores every county and cohort, then sorts them into High, Medium, and Low tiers built on calibrated probabilities, so outreach starts where the need is greatest.

Why

Plain-language drivers

Every score comes with the factors behind it: food access, care access, provider shortage, rurality, and more. No black boxes, just the reasons a community ranks where it does.

What next

Reviewable next steps

Each risk tier maps to realistic preventive actions a small clinic can take, and a provider accepts or overrides every recommendation. The atlas informs; a person decides.

The atlas informs. A provider decides.
The working principle behind every recommendation in the system.
  1. 01

    Grounded in public data

    County indicators come from Census ACS, CDC PLACES, HRSA shortage areas, EPA EJSCREEN, and USDA food-access data. Clinical records are synthetic or de-identified, never live PHI.

  2. 02

    Modeled with care

    Features are screened for proxy bias before training, and models are judged on calibration and recall for the communities that matter, with fairness across rurality checked explicitly.

  3. 03

    Explained, not asserted

    Every risk score ships with its drivers in plain language, so a care coordinator can see why a county or cohort ranks where it does before acting on it.

  4. 04

    Human in the loop

    Recommended actions stay recommendations until a provider accepts or overrides them, and every override is recorded. No clinical decision is ever automated.
